Money matters more in GTA 6 than in any previous GTA. Some story missions require you to have a certain amount of cash before you can continue, so earning money is part of progression, not just a side goal. The best method depends on how far you are in the story: each chapter opens up new types of robberies and better fences who pay more for stolen goods.

## How money works

Cash you carry is separate from money in the bank. If you are Wasted or Busted, you lose the cash on your person, so deposit your earnings at an ATM regularly. Stolen goods such as jewelry, drugs and marked bills are not spendable until you sell them to a fence, and each fence pays best for their own specialty.

- **Brian and Donnie** (Chapter 1, southern Key Lento) buy early loot and pay cash into your home mailbox.
- **Moses and Dunk** (Chapter 2, Port Gellhorn) handle stolen vehicles.
- **Tyrone** (Chapter 3, La Perle) pays well for narcotics.
- **Yasmani** (Chapter 3, Little Cuba) pays well for jewelry and marked bills.
- **Randy** (Chapter 3, Balsero) deals in high-end cars.

## Chapter 1: don't farm

Farming money in Chapter 1 is pointless: you lose your cash at the end of the chapter anyway. Focus on the story, and grab what comes naturally along the way.

- Rob stores by aiming a gun at the clerk and emptying the register.
- Hit gang operations, marked by yellow icons on the map. There are 39 across Leonida.
- Win races. There are 40 unique race locations.

## Chapter 2: sell cars to Moses

Early in Chapter 2, after the main mission where you take a vehicle to Moses, you can sell stolen cars to him at Watkins Auto Parts in Port Gellhorn. He only accepts cars without trackers, and typically pays $1,000 or more per car for a couple of minutes of work. There is a short cooldown of around two minutes between sales, and he will not take cars stolen too close to his garage, so grab vehicles from the main road in Port Gellhorn instead.

## Chapter 3: jewelry, loans and banks

Chapter 3 is where money-making opens up properly. You also receive $120,000 automatically from story progress at the end of the chapter.

- **Jewelry heists** pay up to $25,000 per store. There are 35 pawn shops and jewelry stores in total.
- **Payday loan jobs** (7 in total) pay roughly $2,000 to $25,000.
- **Small banks** (14 in total) pay roughly $5,000 to $45,000.
- **Gang hideouts** hold drugs, watches and safes. Expensive drugs such as crystal and cocaine sell for far more than weed.

One warning: bank cash carries dye packs that explode if you get too far away. You get a dye pack jammer during a Chapter 3 mission, so hold off on banks until then.

## Chapter 4 and endgame: bank vaults

The biggest payouts in the game come from large bank vaults, unlocked at the end of Chapter 4 through the optional quest "That's Where The Money Is..." (wait at the cafe). Follow a security van until it stops at a bank, then slip into the vault while the guard carries money inside. Vaults pay from around $65,000 up to $205,000, with the Lombank vaults in Downtown and Ocean Beach paying the most. Robbing a vault instantly gives you a 4-star wanted level, so bring a fast motorcycle for the getaway and switch vehicles once you escape.

After the quest, Yasmani sells tips about security van routes for $10,000, provided you have already done his two payday loan jobs. In the endgame you can repeat vault robberies for roughly $100,000 to $200,000 every few minutes.

## Other income

- **Treasure diving for Kenton:** the old diver in Key Lento marks treasure spots on the ocean floor. Finding every piece at each dive site eventually earns tens of thousands of dollars.
- **Story missions:** main missions regularly pay out large chunks of cash, so pushing the story forward is always a reliable earner.
- **Mugging pedestrians** pays very little. It is barely enough for a snack, so don't bother.
