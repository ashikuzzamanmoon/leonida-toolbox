Vehicle customization in GTA 6 is split between shops, and it goes deeper than GTA 5: interiors can be changed down to the stitching on the seats, and performance parts show their effect on speed and acceleration before you buy. The first rule is to register the car before spending money on it, or you risk losing the whole build.

## Where to customize

- **Pay 'n' Spray** handles repairs and a basic color change. That is all it does: pick from a small set of colors and drive out. A respray also helps you lose the police, since it changes the vehicle description they are looking for. Pay 'n' Sprays unlock through story progression, so they are not available from the very start.
- **Mod shops** are the game's full customization garages. Coastal Auto Care in Key Lento is one every copy of the game has. The menu splits into six tabs: Pro Builds, Cosmetics, Bodywork, Performance, Wheels and Interior.
- **Rideout Customs** in Vice City and **One-Eyed Willie's** at Lake Leonida are specialist mod shops locked to the Ultimate Edition. Rideout focuses on interiors, rims and donk styling; One-Eyed Willie's does off-road builds and hand-painted finishes.
- **Gas stations** can also repair your vehicle.

Specialty garages are marked on the map while you are driving a compatible vehicle, and they offer exclusive cosmetic and bodywork mods for it.

## Performance upgrades

The Performance tab covers the parts that change how the car drives: full engine upgrades, intakes, transmission and boost (nitrous). Prices seen at Coastal Auto Care on launch night include:

| Upgrade | Price | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine Upgrade Stage 1 | $900 | +0.3 speed, +0.3 acceleration |
| Transmission Upgrade Level 1 | $600 | Improved shifting |
| Exposed Air Filter (Intakes) | $750 | Small performance gain |
| Nitrous | $5,000 | Boost |

You can rev the engine in the shop before buying a part, so you can hear what it does. Major upgrades require money in a bank account rather than cash in your pocket. Exhaust parts also add small performance gains: every exhaust inspected at Coastal Auto Care added 0.1 to both speed and acceleration.

## Looks: bodywork, paint and wheels

- **Bodywork** covers bumpers, spoilers, tow hitches, chrome mirrors, window tint, headlight color and roof racks or camper shells for truck beds. An Overriders front bumper costs $390; hood catches run $275 to $325.
- **Paint and liveries** include resprays sorted by color family ($400), vintage stripes ($740) and retro liveries ($750). Some liveries glow in the dark, and exhaust flames can be color-customized.
- **Wheels** offer new tires with many rim sizes, retro and '90s rims, and finishes in gloss, metallic or matching paint. Even lug nut color can be changed.
- **License plates** for Leonida come in School, Sport and Wildlife designs for $55.
- **Pro Builds** are full kits. A Retro Build, with custom paint, interior and a modded engine, costs $2,930 and requires a bank account.

## Interiors

Interior customization is the deepest it has ever been in GTA. You can change steering wheels (a Retro 3 Spoke wheel is $280), fit bucket seats in leather or cloth (premium seats $600), and customize upholstery color, stitching and trim. Interior LED strips and lighting colors are also available.

## Restoring wrecks and keeping your build

- **Rusted-out project cars** can be restored: bodywork ($200), engine bay ($50), wheels ($100) and interior ($100).
- **Register first.** Only registered cars are truly yours. See [Best Cars in GTA 6](/guides/best-cars) for how registration works.
- **Repair before modding.** The shop asks you to repair the vehicle before making modifications, and at Coastal Auto Care the repair was free.
- **Garage space matters.** Every registered car needs a free slot. See [Businesses & Properties in GTA 6](/guides/businesses-properties) for garages.
