GTA 6 launched on PlayStation 5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S. Every version targets 30 frames per second, and there is no 60fps or performance mode on any console. Beyond that, the versions differ in resolution, upscaling and how steadily they hold that 30fps target. The details below come from Digital Foundry's launch testing across all four consoles.

## At a glance

| Console | Internal resolution | Upscaling | Ray tracing | Frame rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PS5 Pro | 1440p | 4K via PSSR | GI, reflections and shadows | Near-flawless 30fps |
| PS5 | 1440p | 4K via Rockstar's upscaler | GI and reflections | Small dips in heavy scenes |
| Xbox Series X | 1440p | 4K via Rockstar's upscaler | GI and reflections | Mostly 30fps, dips in heavy fights |
| Xbox Series S | 720p | 1080p via FSR 4 | GI and reflections, lower quality | Often below 30fps |

## PS5 vs Xbox Series X: nearly identical

The base PS5 and the Xbox Series X render the game the same way: 1440p internally, upscaled to 4K with Rockstar's own temporal upscaler, with ray-traced global illumination and reflections. In practice the two look and play almost identically.

Both hold 30fps most of the time and dip in the same kinds of scenes: fast chases, big shootouts and explosions. In stress tests, the base PS5 dropped to around 23fps in a car-destruction test, while the Series X dropped to around 20 to 21fps in a busy police shootout. Those were different tests, so they show the consoles are close rather than giving a precise ranking. Digital Foundry's verdict on the gap was short: the difference is not huge.

One real difference is variable refresh rate. Xbox consoles can run the game in a 120Hz VRR container, which smooths out dropped frames. PlayStation's system-level VRR only works above 48fps and has no low frame-rate compensation, so it does nothing in a 30fps game.

## PS5 Pro: the best way to play on console

The PS5 Pro is the clearest winner. It renders at the same 1440p, but upscales to 4K with PSSR, Sony's AI upscaler, which Digital Foundry describes as convincingly close to native 4K with a much cleaner image in the distance. It is also the only console with ray-traced shadows, on top of the global illumination and reflections every version has.

It holds 30fps almost perfectly. Across more than 50 hours of testing, Digital Foundry saw no dips in normal play. Only a deliberate stress test, blowing up cars with a grenade launcher, brought it down to 24fps, and it recovered quickly.

## Xbox Series S: the weakest version

The Series S has the hardest job and it shows. It renders at 720p, upscaled to about 1080p with AMD's FSR 4, with lower-quality ray tracing. It also holds 30fps the loosest: long stretches of ordinary play run in the mid to high 20s, and the worst fights and chases drop to 18 or 19fps. Digital Foundry described it as having serious frame-rate problems. It is still the full game, but it is noticeably the roughest way to play it.

## Which console should you buy it on?

- **PS5 Pro owners** get the best console version by a clear margin: sharpest image, extra ray tracing and the steadiest frame rate.
- **Base PS5 and Series X owners** get effectively the same experience. Buy it on the console you already own, or where your friends play.
- **Series S owners** should know what they are getting: a softer image and frequent dips below 30fps. If you have access to another console, use it.
- **DualSense fans** may prefer PlayStation: GTA 6 makes heavy use of the DualSense controller's features, which several reviewers singled out.
